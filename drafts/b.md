OPEN INTEREST / Daily brief, format v2. Thursday, 26 November 2026.

LEAD: Forged internet routes diverted bitcoin hashrate from more than a thousand mining sites.

Braiins says an attacker hijacked unencrypted Stratum V1 connections and pointed miners' work at an imposter pool. The flaw is as old as the protocol, and the attack was still live on 25 November 2026.

On 25 November 2026 the mining pool operator Braiins published a security alert describing a hashrate theft that touched neither the miners nor its own systems [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. The attacker rerouted traffic between the two, impersonated the pool and fed the miners its own jobs [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1].

The scale grew quickly. Braiins counted 19 episodes in Latin America between 7 and 12 November 2026, each hitting 120 to 400 sites and 36 to 174 PH/s [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. On 20 November 2026 four episodes, mostly in Europe, hit 1,050 to 1,600 sites and up to 1.9 EH/s, roughly ten times the largest earlier wave [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. Each lasted between 6 and 49 minutes [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1].

The method was a routing trick. The pool's provider announces a block of 4,096 addresses, and somebody announced a more specific block of 256 that contained the pool's address [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. Networks that do not check routes against the RPKI registry followed the forged sign [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. The forgery also named the real owner as its origin, so origin-only hijack monitors stayed quiet [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1].

The imposter answered a Brazilian miner in about 30 ms, against about 280 ms for the real server, and accepted any password [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. It relayed jobs from a different large public pool and submitted the shares under its own account [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. Braiins does not name that pool and calls it a destination, not a party to the attack [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1].

The weakness is not new. Dell SecureWorks documented the same routing trick taking about $83,000 in rewards in 2014, Braiins notes [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. Braiins says forged announcements also covered blocks hosting other large pools' endpoints, some still on 22 November 2026 [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. Its prescription is Stratum V2, Stratum V1 over verified TLS, or a local proxy it released the same day [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1]. The alert gives no estimate of the bitcoin stolen [https://braiins.com/blog/bitcoin-hashrate-hijacking-unencrypted-stratum-v1].

NEWS:

[NEWS] Chainflip lost about $12.6m after an attacker signed vault withdrawals outside the network.
Chainflip Labs says an attacker took 133.34 BTC, 520.22 ETH, 3,249,631 USDC and 758,141 USDT on 21 November 2026, effectively each vault's entire balance [https://blog.chainflip.io/incident-report-vault-exploit-21-november-2026-part-1]. The attacker registered a validator with the minimum stake, used peer connections to exploit a memory-safety flaw in a third-party networking library and extracted key shares, though the team says some steps are not yet confirmed [https://blog.chainflip.io/incident-report-vault-exploit-21-november-2026-part-1]. The first theft is Bitcoin transaction 20262de39ec3331d78be2b87f18c90cb2ee30363311dd43def14c3aecf155b7a, and 2,909 ETH went into Tornado Cash within minutes [https://blog.chainflip.io/incident-report-vault-exploit-21-november-2026-part-1]. An internal monitor flagged the first outflow at 06:49 UTC, eight minutes in, but was not wired to escalation, and a further 10.48 BTC left the vault at 11:24 UTC [https://blog.chainflip.io/incident-report-vault-exploit-21-november-2026-part-1].

[NEWS] SEC staff will not pursue Molecule's unregistered sale of biotech research tokens.
The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance sent a no-action letter dated 24 November 2026 to Molecule, a decentralised-science company, crypto.news reports [https://crypto.news/molecule-sec-no-action-letter-biotech-tokens/]. Staff said they would not recommend enforcement if Molecule's US unit sells its project tokens without registering them, according to the same report [https://crypto.news/molecule-sec-no-action-letter-biotech-tokens/]. CoinMarketCap's account of the request says the tokens carry no equity or debt interest and give lockers only advisory votes [https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/crypto-news-today-nov-25-2026]. The letter states it expresses an enforcement position only and no legal conclusion, CoinMarketCap quotes [https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/crypto-news-today-nov-25-2026].

[NEWS] Ukraine's parliament voted to legalise and tax virtual assets from 1 July 2027.
The Verkhovna Rada passed bill No. 10225-d in its final reading on 25 November 2026 by 271 votes to 8, Interfax-Ukraine reports [https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/economic/1242107.html]. Individuals will pay tax on annual profit only, with crypto-to-crypto swaps and gains under one minimum wage exempt, and assets already held get a 5% income-tax rate through 2028 [https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/economic/1242107.html]. CoinMarketCap puts the standard rate at 23%, an 18% income tax plus a 5% military levy, and says the law still needs the president's signature [https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/crypto-news-today-nov-25-2026]. Exchanges and custodians must obtain authorisation, register within 60 days and report annually on Ukrainian residents' transactions [https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/economic/1242107.html].

TECH:

[TECH] Ethlabs recommends shipping Glamsterdam with its disruptive state repricing intact.
Caspar Schwarz-Schilling wrote on 25 November 2026 that Ethlabs backs Glamsterdam as is, while calling the problems found serious and valid [https://ethlabs.org/writings/glamsterdam-position.html]. Most of the disruption comes from EIP-8037, which makes state creation more expensive, and pulling it would give up a gas limit roughly three times higher [https://ethlabs.org/writings/glamsterdam-position.html]. He argues that any change now adds months, and that the Sepolia testnet already forked with the current version [https://ethlabs.org/writings/glamsterdam-position.html]. The note asks future forks to flag disruptive changes early and bring application developers in sooner [https://ethlabs.org/writings/glamsterdam-position.html].

[TECH] Trail of Bits says a valid signature can hand an attacker standing wallet authority.
Trail of Bits argued on 6 November 2026 that the Binance theft shows a compromised server can misuse a protected key without ever extracting it [https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/11/06/after-binance-check-what-your-signing-systems-can-approve/]. The attacker used an EIP-7702 delegation, a standing instruction that stays on an account until the key clears it, to keep moving funds deposited after the first theft [https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/11/06/after-binance-check-what-your-signing-systems-can-approve/]. The firm warns that monitors watching only transfers and ownership changes miss such authority shifts [https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/11/06/after-binance-check-what-your-signing-systems-can-approve/]. Its first step for custodians is to document every service that can request signatures and what each can authorise [https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/11/06/after-binance-check-what-your-signing-systems-can-approve/].

[CULTURE] An Ethereum core developer pitches the next two forks as a store-of-value programme.
Derek Chiang of Ethlabs wrote on 22 November 2026 that core developers hold ETH and feel responsible when markets fall [https://ethlabs.org/updates/week-22.html]. He frames Glamsterdam as scaling without losing decentralisation, and Hegota as protecting the chain against quantum computers and AI [https://ethlabs.org/updates/week-22.html]. He argues that ETH's value as a store does not depend on fee revenue, which falls per transaction as the chain scales [https://ethlabs.org/updates/week-22.html]. He says core developers insist on keeping EIP-8037 in Glamsterdam despite its issues because it keeps the network decentralised at higher scale [https://ethlabs.org/updates/week-22.html].
